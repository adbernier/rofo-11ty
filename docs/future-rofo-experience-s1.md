# Future Rofo Experience Notes

Sprint S1 established the homepage and San Francisco as the working prototype for Rofo's future product experience. The product should stay simple on the surface: start a search, review an initial location analysis, understand the tradeoffs, then choose whether to refine, save, or request help.

## Progressive Analysis Model

Future intake questions should only be added when the answer can visibly improve the analysis. Rofo should not add fields simply because a broker or CRM would eventually like the information.

Potential refinement inputs:

- Timing
- Rough budget
- Growth plans
- Employee commute patterns
- Customer visit frequency
- Parking requirements
- Transit importance
- Image versus cost tolerance
- Operational requirements

Each added question should improve at least one visible outcome:

- Better district prioritization
- Clearer tradeoffs
- Stronger validation questions
- More relevant representative buildings
- Better next-step options

## Future Marketplace Direction

Rofo may eventually support multiple post-analysis outcomes depending on market coverage, partner availability, landlord relationships, profile quality, and current inventory access.

Potential models:

- Tenant representatives receive a prepared, qualified business profile.
- Landlord representatives offer relevant properties or tours.
- Listing brokers receive an anonymous requirement profile and submit a listing URL, a concise fit explanation, and optional availability or tour information.
- Rofo reviews or organizes submitted opportunities before presenting them to the business.
- Different next steps appear based on market support, profile quality, and partner coverage.

Customer identity and contact information should remain protected until sharing is intentional. Rofo should remain the trusted organizational layer and should not expose users to an uncontrolled flow of broker responses.

## Learning Gaps

S1 did not add analytics events. Future instrumentation should measure whether the prototype journey is working before broad rollout:

- Homepage product CTA clicks
- San Francisco hero CTA clicks
- Chapter navigation engagement
- District link engagement
- Comparison engagement
- Example building engagement
- Handbook topic engagement
- Find Locations starts
- Recommendation completion
- Analysis save or email actions
- Expert review or broker introduction requests

Broader rollout of the San Francisco format should wait until these signals show that the prototype improves product entry, recommendation completion, and meaningful engagement with district, handbook, and building context.
