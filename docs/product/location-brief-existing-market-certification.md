# Existing-Market Location Brief Certification

## Executive Answer

The shared six-section Location Brief works across all four existing bounded Industrial/Flex flows without market-specific Brief code. North Orange County, Phoenix, Indianapolis, and Sacramento each produced a recognizable search assignment, preserved the certified recommendation universe, and gave tenant and supply-side readers a useful starting point.

The first pass exposed the same three shared presentation defects in all four markets: unmapped activity IDs became phrases such as “ship distribute” or “make assemble”; the district sentence wrapper produced bad articles for some governed distinctions; and `PREFERRED` operational criteria were phrased too strongly as things the space “needs to support.” These were **universal product issues**, not Recommendation Intelligence or market-intelligence failures.

The shared projection now preserves canonical strength, translates every currently supported Requirement activity used by the Brief, omits unknown activity IDs rather than exposing them, and uses a grammar-neutral district distinction wrapper. The exact same eight Requirements were recertified with identical recommendation fingerprints before and after presentation. North Orange County, Phoenix, Indianapolis, and Sacramento are now **READY**.

The scalability result is positive. The six-section projection is genuinely shared. Market differences enter through bounded membership, evidence-backed candidate facts, adapters, composition, and activation—not through separate Location Brief renderers. The next constraint is that new Industrial/Flex markets still require bespoke adapter and composition modules. A governed Market Pack is supported as a direction, but this audit does not justify a wholesale refactor.

## Final Recertification Result

The shared corrections were:

- `REQUIRED` property/operational facts may join the “needs to support” sentence.
- `PREFERRED` property/operational facts receive a separate “We'd prefer …” sentence and never enter the required sentence.
- `FLEXIBLE` facts remain under “What we're flexible about.”
- Unknown or unanswered facts produce no property claim and never become flexibility.
- Canonical activities now use governed human labels such as “shipping and distribution,” “manufacturing and assembly,” “prototyping,” and “research and development.” An unknown activity has no fallback that can expose its machine ID.
- District distinctions now follow a colon or dash structure that accepts noun phrases, plurals, and possessives without generated articles or forced lowercasing.

For all eight Requirements, the before/after recommendation fingerprint was identical: readiness, eligible candidates, shortlist, ordering, composition, intelligence gaps, and product response did not change. All eight remained non-abstaining, municipality-contained, and inside their certified candidate universes.

Representative corrected output includes:

- “The space needs to support storage, receiving and truck circulation, as well as shipping and distribution. We'd prefer grade-level loading.”
- “The space needs to support R&D, prototyping and day-to-day work, as well as manufacturing and assembly.”
- “We'd start with Power Inn: Sacramento's deeper warehouse, distribution and production district.”

## Universal Product Findings

What worked everywhere:

- The same six-section projection accepted every market's canonical Requirement and recommendation snapshot.
- Business, size, operations, flexibility, priorities, and bounded shortlist were preserved.
- One-location and two-location states both read as coherent assignments.
- Unknown flexibility did not become flexibility.
- Municipality containment and default-deny behavior remained below the presentation layer.
- The primary Brief can serve tenant, tenant broker, and supply-side readers when the Requirement is sufficiently complete.

Universal defects found and closed by recertification:

1. **Activity-label leakage — closed.** Known canonical activities have explicit shared customer labels; unknown IDs are omitted from primary prose.
2. **Status overstatement in “Likely space” — closed.** Required and preferred operational criteria are composed separately and tested independently from flexible and unanswered states.
3. **District grammar assumptions — closed.** The common wrapper no longer manufactures articles or lowercases proper distinctions.
4. **Requirement-specific connection is mostly implicit.** “Where we'd start” uses the correct Requirement-driven shortlist and useful district distinctions, but it does not always state the connecting reasoning as directly as a broker would. This is acceptable in the strongest cases but should be improved through shared composition, not market copy.

## Repeated Market Work

The repeated work for each Industrial/Flex market is:

1. Define the owned municipality and supported candidate universe.
2. Review evidence for each candidate's commercial role and distinctions.
3. Map common Requirement activities into Industrial, Flex, or mixed intent.
4. Encode abstention for unsupported geography, specialized use, decisive property capabilities, and unresolved access.
5. Define composition and ordering across candidates.
6. Register certification and bounded activation.
7. Build market-specific QA for membership, neutrality, abstention, recommendation states, rendering, and evidence containment.

Steps 1, 2, 5, and much of 6–7 are inherently governed market knowledge and certification. Step 3 and the shared abstention categories are substantially repeated software.

## Can Market Knowledge Replace Market Software?

**Mostly, but not with the repository exactly as it stands.** The audit supports a separation in which market knowledge supplies geography, evidence, distinctions, supported signals, and explicit boundaries while shared software handles the six-section Brief, common status semantics, activation, and QA patterns.

Today, adding the next Industrial/Flex market still requires a new adapter and composition module even when they repeat established patterns. Some software work therefore remains. The main reasons are:

- market membership and alias normalization are encoded in code;
- intent keywords and candidate activation are encoded per adapter/composer;
- abstention rules have common structure but separate implementations;
- QA invokes market-specific modules and assertions.

The current evidence is sufficient to test a declarative market contract. It is not sufficient to replace all adapters immediately.

## Proposed Repeatable Market Contract

A minimal governed Industrial/Flex Market Pack should contain:

1. Market ID, municipality ownership, aliases, and explicit excluded municipalities.
2. Supported property type and bounded candidate IDs.
3. Candidate names, reviewed distinctions, operating traits, and comparison roles.
4. Requirement signals that activate Industrial, Flex, mixed, or candidate-specific traits.
5. Common abstention inputs plus any reviewed market-specific unsupported boundaries.
6. Representative environments or properties, with their existing non-availability semantics.
7. Optional reviewed access facts, kept separate from recommendation effects unless certified.
8. Source provenance, review status, certification ID, and activation eligibility.
9. Deterministic fixtures for one-peer, multi-peer, and investigate states.

Shared software should continue to own Requirement-status interpretation, recommendation-state semantics, customer-language projection, Location Brief rendering, default-deny activation, and reusable certification assertions. Candidate ordering should become data-driven only where the current composition rules prove structurally equivalent; genuine market-specific logic should remain explicit.

## Future Market Certification Standard

A future market is ready only when it demonstrates:

- a bounded, municipality-safe supported geography;
- reviewed evidence and useful distinctions for every eligible candidate;
- at least two materially different Requirements producing meaningfully different results where the market supports them;
- one honest abstention for insufficient or unsupported input;
- one-location and multi-location states where applicable;
- a six-section Brief with no enum/activity leakage or semantic status overstatement;
- tenant, tenant-broker, and landlord/listing-broker readability;
- preserved provenance and representative-only semantics;
- desktop and mobile rendering;
- default-deny activation and an explicit certification record;
- deterministic regression QA proving no out-of-universe recommendation.

Passing page rendering alone is insufficient.
