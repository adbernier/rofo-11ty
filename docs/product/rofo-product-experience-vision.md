# Rofo Product Experience (PX) Vision

**Status:** Product Vision
**Owner:** Product
**Audience:** Engineering, Design, Editorial, AI/Codex

---

# Purpose

Rofo has reached a new stage of maturity.

The core challenge is no longer building commercial real estate knowledge. We have established a strong editorial foundation through district relationships, representative buildings, commercial ecosystems, photography, explainability, and the Commercial Location Knowledge Graph.

Our challenge now is transforming that knowledge into a product that feels effortless, valuable, and trustworthy.

This document defines the product philosophy that should guide every future design and engineering decision.

---

# Mission

**Rofo helps businesses decide where they should begin looking for commercial space—and shows them why.**

Rofo is fundamentally a recommendation product.

It is **not** primarily:

* a listing website
* a commercial real estate directory
* a broker marketplace
* a commercial real estate encyclopedia

Those capabilities support the recommendation.

They are not the product.

---

# Product Promise

When someone lands on Rofo they should immediately understand:

> **"Rofo will recommend where my business should search for space."**

The experience should feel like receiving guidance from an experienced commercial real estate advisor.

---

# The User Journey

## Step 1 — Understand the Promise

The homepage has one responsibility.

Explain the product.

The visitor should immediately understand:

* what Rofo does
* what they will receive
* why it is different
* what action they should take

Everything else is secondary.

---

## Step 2 — Understand My Business

The Search Profile exists to understand the user's business.

It should feel conversational rather than administrative.

When complete, the user should think:

> "Rofo understands my business."

---

## Step 3 — Receive a Recommendation

This is the product.

Rofo recommends:

* markets
* districts
* neighborhoods

The recommendation should be concise, confident, and immediately useful.

Example:

**Recommended**

1. Mission Bay
2. Dogpatch
3. Potrero Hill

Immediately followed by:

> Here's why.

---

## Step 4 — Location Brief

The Location Brief is the signature deliverable of Rofo.

Everything we build should strengthen this document.

A Location Brief includes:

* recommendation summary
* reasoning
* business fit
* representative buildings
* district character
* commercial ecosystem
* nearby relationships
* transportation
* tradeoffs
* photography
* editorial insight

The Location Brief should be valuable enough that users want to:

* save it
* print it
* share it
* forward it to partners
* discuss it internally

It should feel like professional advisory work.

---

## Step 5 — Execute

Only after confidence has been established should users begin searching available inventory.

Broker engagement exists to execute the recommendation—not replace it.

The broker receives:

* business profile
* recommendations
* Location Brief
* requirements
* unresolved questions

The broker starts informed.

---

# Design Principles

## Every page has one job.

Homepage

Explain the product.

Search Profile

Understand the business.

Recommendation

Tell me where.

Location Brief

Explain why.

Broker

Help me execute.

If a page attempts multiple primary jobs, simplify it.

---

## Recommendation Before Inventory

Users first understand:

Where.

Then:

Why.

Only afterward:

Available space.

Inventory should reinforce confidence rather than distract from it.

---

## Evidence Over Marketing

Avoid making unsupported marketing claims.

Instead of saying:

"We know the market."

Show:

* representative buildings
* district relationships
* commercial ecosystems
* neighborhood comparisons
* editorial observations
* photography

Trust should be earned through evidence.

---

## Simplicity Wins

Every screen should reduce cognitive load.

Remove unnecessary navigation.

Reduce competing calls to action.

Guide users naturally toward the next step.

Every click should move the experience forward.

---

## Internal Systems Remain Internal

The following are implementation systems.

They improve recommendations but should not become user-facing concepts.

Examples:

* EOS
* Commercial Location Knowledge Graph
* Editorial Health
* Representative Building scoring
* Coverage metrics
* Confidence calculations

Users experience better recommendations.

They do not need to understand the infrastructure.

---

# Homepage Vision

The homepage becomes a product landing page.

Primary headline:

> Get a personalized recommendation for where your business should search for commercial space.

Primary CTA:

Start Your Search

Supporting visual:

Business Profile

↓

Location Recommendation

↓

Location Brief

↓

Broker Partnership

The homepage should also demonstrate the product through:

* an example Location Brief
* representative recommendations
* a simple explanation of the process
* trust-building evidence
* a single, obvious next action

---

# City Page Vision

City pages become localized recommendation experiences.

They answer questions like:

* What businesses thrive here?
* Which districts fit different business types?
* How do neighborhoods compare?
* Where should I begin?

Each city page should feature:

* an example city-specific Location Brief
* representative district recommendations
* representative buildings
* commercial context
* a clear path into the Search Profile

---

# Broker Experience

Rofo should create better broker conversations.

Instead of delivering a generic lead, Rofo delivers context.

Every broker engagement should begin with:

Business Profile

Recommendation

Location Brief

Requirements

Questions still unresolved

The broker's time should focus on execution rather than discovery.

---

# Product Principles

Before adding any new feature, ask:

Does this improve the Location Brief?

Does it improve recommendation quality?

Does it increase user confidence?

Does it simplify the experience?

Does it improve broker execution?

If the answer is no, it is likely outside the core product.

---

# Success Metric

Rofo succeeds when users say:

> "Rofo recommended places I hadn't considered, and now I understand exactly why."

Not:

> "Rofo has a lot of information."

The product should be remembered for clarity, confidence, and trusted recommendations.

---

# Current Product Priorities

The next phase of development focuses on product experience rather than expanding capability.

Priority order:

1. Rewrite the homepage around the recommendation promise.
2. Introduce polished example Location Briefs on the homepage and city pages.
3. Simplify the information architecture so every page has a single purpose.
4. Strengthen the recommendation experience before exposing inventory.
5. Redesign the broker handoff around context rather than lead generation.
6. Refine navigation, messaging, and visual hierarchy to create a calm, intuitive experience.
7. Continue improving recommendation quality behind the scenes through EOS, editorial work, and the Commercial Location Knowledge Graph.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every decision should reinforce one simple idea:

**Rofo is a recommendation product, not a search product.**

If future design or engineering decisions conflict with this principle, the recommendation experience should take precedence.
