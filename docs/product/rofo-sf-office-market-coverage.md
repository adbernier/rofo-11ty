## Access foundation

Access remains a graph, not a district-by-origin score table:

`origin region → reviewed gateway/system → reviewed district relationship → bounded district Access result`

Reviewed systems include BART, Caltrain, ferry, Golden Gate Bridge, Bay Bridge, US-101, I-280, local transit, and the central street network. Parking is a bounded modifier on a supported driving path; it cannot create geographic access by itself. Exact commute time, traffic, station walking time, and building parking remain out of scope.

Changes in this sprint:

- Added a reviewed Dogpatch Access profile using existing official SFMTA, SF Planning, SFCTA, and Caltrain evidence. It supports local transit, 22nd Street Caltrain, I-280/US-101 structural relationships, bounded parking, provenance, and explicit North Bay/block-level unknowns.
- Expanded the canonical `Across the Bay Area / mixed` Requirement value into five material cohorts: San Francisco, East Bay, Marin/North Bay, Peninsula, and South Bay. It no longer disappears as opaque unresolved text.
- Corrected the composition foundation's Commercial Location Model adapter to read the model's `byPath` index. Reviewed CLM context and provenance now reach the foundation again.

No Requirement-to-district table, numeric averaging, or scenario-specific recommendation rule was added.

## Parking and transit

Parking remains an arrival modifier and explanation dimension, never a substitute for a reviewed origin path. Helpful/medium preferences can distinguish explanations without forcing a rank movement. Transit uses the actual supported regional/local systems; a generic `transit: high` label does not manufacture an East Bay BART path.

This discipline is why several scenarios remain BOUNDED rather than being cosmetically upgraded to FULL.

## Sensitivity findings

- **East Bay → Marin:** the same reusable gateway graph changes the defensible guidance from BART-oriented downtown choices toward Presidio/northern access consideration. Both remain BOUNDED for different missing paths.
- **Transit important → parking important:** parking modifies only reviewed driving paths. It changes evidence and tradeoffs, not eligibility by itself.
- **Professional services → architecture/design:** Access and Office components remain identical when their inputs are identical; only reviewed Business Environment activation changes the universe and explanations.
- **No candidate → Mission Bay candidate:** readiness, component results, ordering, and eligibility remain identical. Only candidate assessment/presentation changes.
- Not every meaningful answer changes order. Several cases correctly retain an order while changing the explanation or confidence boundary.

## Scalability assessment

**Yes, the foundation pattern can be populated for Denver, Seattle, Los Angeles, or another unfamiliar market without a bespoke recommendation model.** The reusable logic is:

- canonical decision geography and presentation grouping;
- property-type-specific Office Fit;
- Business Environment characteristics/priors;
- origin→gateway→district Access graph;
- Requirement-specific readiness and structured gaps;
- component composition with candidate neutrality.

SF-specific facts—BART, Caltrain, bridges, district identities, and local parking environments—are legitimate market data. The gateway derivation, cohort integrity, presentation grouping, readiness, and composition are reusable model logic.

The remaining problematic market-specific logic is the older `_data/sfOfficeRecommendationModel.js` `rise`/`enter` machinery and production resolver ordering. It is still used as reviewed candidate activation/tie context. It should eventually be expressed through normalized market foundation characteristics, but it was not refactored in this sprint and no new scenario rules were added.
