# Bay Area Commercial Geography Expansion Plan V1

Generated: 2026-05-21

This is a strategic planning artifact for Rofo's Bay Area commercial geography system. It does not generate pages, create compare pages, alter templates, expose graph data publicly, or change live behavior.

## Strategic Frame

The Bay Area should become Rofo's flagship commercial geography ecosystem because it has unusually strong district differentiation: traditional CBDs, mixed-use urban districts, waterfront commercial areas, adaptive industrial districts, life-science corridors, startup and R&D corridors, and logistics corridors all sit close enough to create meaningful comparison paths.

The goal is not volume. The goal is a dense, credible Bay Area district graph where each public district has:

- a clear commercial identity
- practical tenant orientation
- useful nearby district relationships
- representative built-form examples
- restrained editorial interpretation
- strong map and image support
- enough evidence to avoid thin SEO pages

## Expansion Principles

1. Build district depth before geographic breadth.
2. Treat commercial districts as business environments, not arbitrary polygons.
3. Use corridor language only when the geography is access-led or linear.
4. Separate city-level identity from district-level identity.
5. Keep representative buildings as examples, not the intelligence source.
6. Do not publish district relationships until editorial review confirms the public rationale.
7. Avoid rent, vacancy, ranking, inventory, or "best" claims.
8. Use public terminology centered on commercial districts, business districts, nearby districts, related districts, and selective commercial corridors.

## Public Integration Rules

Before any candidate becomes public:

1. It must have a District Graph node.
2. It must have reviewed relationships to nearby or related commercial districts.
3. It must have a short editorial interpretation focused on identity, tenant orientation, access, and built form.
4. It must have representative examples that support the district texture.
5. It must have map/image requirements.
6. It must avoid scores, rankings, rent, vacancy, inventory depth, and current availability claims.
7. It must use commercial district, business district, nearby district, related district, or commercial corridor language.

## Summary

The Bay Area expansion should be built as a district graph, not a page factory. Rofo should deepen the Bay Area through a small number of high-quality commercial districts and corridors that explain how businesses compare place, access, built form, and commercial identity.

The strongest near-term opportunity is to make the Bay Area feel like a connected commercial geography system: Oakland core districts, San Francisco business and mixed-use districts, Peninsula downtowns, biotech corridors, South Bay R&D corridors, and East Bay industrial/logistics corridors. Each should be added only when the evidence supports a clear and useful district interpretation.
