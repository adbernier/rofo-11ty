# Bay Area Tier A District Manifest

This is a curated planning manifest for the first Bay Area commercial geography cluster. It treats the rollout targets as commercial districts rather than raw legacy neighborhood labels. No pages were generated in this pass.

## Audit summary

Current public neighborhood pages exist for Downtown Oakland, Uptown Oakland, and Jack London Square. These pages already have commercial-area entities, nearby area relationships, and representative buildings, but they still rely on generic fallback editorial content rather than the Atlanta-style interpretation module.

Downtown Palo Alto, Mountain View / Castro-Whisman, Redwood City Downtown, Downtown San Mateo / Hayward Park, Emeryville / Powell Corridor, and South San Francisco Biotech Corridor do not currently exist as public neighborhood pages in the generated neighborhood page data. They need curated commercial-area entities, editorial naming, nearby comparison sets, and representative building assignment before rollout.

Existing city-level building depth is meaningful across the cluster: Palo Alto has 19 building pages, Mountain View 9, Redwood City 5, San Mateo 7, Oakland 34, Emeryville 1, and South San Francisco 5. Oakland has the strongest district-level assignment today. The Peninsula and South San Francisco have useful city-level inventory, but their raw labels are not clean enough to publish without editorial consolidation.

## Representative building observations

Downtown Oakland has six representative examples and all are office buildings: 610 16th St, 300 Frank H Ogawa Plz, 1440 Broadway, 505 14th St, 1814 Franklin St, and 1300 Clay St. The set is credible for an institutional downtown office page, but visually and typologically it may feel repetitive unless one historic, civic-adjacent, or mixed-use example is introduced later.

Uptown Oakland has six representative examples: 2201 Broadway, 2111 Franklin St, 2101 Webster St, 2100 Franklin St, 2335 Broadway, and 1970 Broadway. The set is very concentrated around Broadway, Franklin, and Webster and reads as office-only. It supports the office district story, but it does not yet show enough mixed-use, arts, retail, or adaptive texture for Uptown's intended positioning.

Jack London Square currently has two representative examples: 66 Franklin St and 230 Madison St. This is directionally useful because it includes office and industrial texture, but it is not enough for a flagship page. The district needs more waterfront, warehouse, brick-and-timber, creative office, or service-commercial examples before it can feel editorially grounded.

Downtown Palo Alto has enough city-level building candidates to begin manual assignment. Hamilton Ave, Cambridge Ave, and nearby El Camino/Park/Birch examples should be separated carefully so Downtown Palo Alto does not accidentally become a broad Palo Alto page.

Mountain View has useful Castro Street and Whisman/Old Middlefield examples, but the district naming decision matters. A single page can work if it is framed as Mountain View / Castro-Whisman, with downtown walkability and R&D adjacency both acknowledged.

Redwood City has a potential mismatch risk: Broadway examples support downtown, while Seaport Blvd and Twin Dolphin Drive point toward waterfront/campus office. That split should become either a comparison note or two separate future districts.

San Mateo has a similar split between downtown examples and Hayward Park/Borel/Concar/Mariners Island office corridors. Combining Downtown San Mateo / Hayward Park is acceptable for the first curated page if the interpretation explicitly says it spans downtown professional services and nearby corridor office.

Emeryville and South San Francisco need manual representative selection before public polish. Emeryville has too little local building depth in the current generated building pages. South San Francisco has enough city-level examples to start, but the available examples do not yet show biotech/lab identity strongly enough.

## Nearby comparison recommendations

- Downtown Palo Alto should compare to Mountain View / Castro-Whisman, Redwood City Downtown, Menlo Park, and California Avenue. The useful decision distinction is premium downtown/client-facing presence versus larger tech/R&D corridors nearby.
- Mountain View / Castro-Whisman should compare to Downtown Palo Alto, Sunnyvale, North San Jose, and Redwood City Downtown. The useful distinction is startup/transit corridor versus premium downtown or larger campus-style office markets.
- Redwood City Downtown should compare to Downtown Palo Alto, Downtown San Mateo / Hayward Park, Menlo Park, and South San Francisco Biotech Corridor. The useful distinction is mid-Peninsula downtown access and civic/Caltrain context.
- Downtown San Mateo / Hayward Park should compare to Redwood City Downtown, Downtown Palo Alto, Foster City, and Burlingame. The useful distinction is practical Peninsula access, medical/professional services, and corridor office options.
- Downtown Oakland should compare to Uptown Oakland, Jack London Square, Old Oakland, Lake Merritt, and the San Francisco Financial District. The useful distinction is institutional downtown value and BART access versus SF pricing and East Bay alternatives.
- Uptown Oakland should compare to Downtown Oakland, Temescal, Lake Merritt, Jack London Square, and Berkeley. The useful distinction is smaller-company/mixed-use context versus formal CBD or neighborhood retail alternatives.
- Jack London Square should compare to Downtown Oakland, Old Oakland, West Oakland, Uptown Oakland, and Alameda. The useful distinction is waterfront/adaptive-commercial identity versus downtown office and industrial-adjacent options.
- Emeryville / Powell Corridor should compare to Downtown Oakland, Berkeley, West Berkeley, Jack London Square, and South San Francisco Biotech Corridor. The useful distinction is compact East Bay access and creative/life science adjacency.
- South San Francisco Biotech Corridor should compare to Mission Bay, Redwood City Downtown, Oyster Point, Brisbane, and Downtown Palo Alto. The useful distinction is lab/flex and airport/101 access versus downtown office or urban life science settings.

## Editorial refinement recommendations

The Oakland pages should be the first content refinement targets because the page infrastructure already exists. Their current live experience still uses generic fallback language such as "At a Glance," "selected commercial buildings," and "Browse selected commercial buildings." That should be replaced with the Atlanta-style editorial interpretation module before these pages are treated as flagships.

Downtown Oakland should emphasize institutional downtown office, BART access, civic and nonprofit/professional services, and relative value compared with San Francisco. It should avoid generic downtown language and make the user understand why a company compares Downtown Oakland with Uptown, Jack London Square, and the SF Financial District.

Uptown Oakland should emphasize mixed-use commercial blocks, smaller-company office fit, arts and food context, and BART-oriented access. It should not read like a second Downtown Oakland page.

Jack London Square should emphasize waterfront identity, warehouse/service-commercial texture, ferry/rail/port adjacency, and creative or operations-adjacent users. It should not be positioned as a generic Oakland office district.

The Peninsula pages need sharper naming before editorial copy is written. Downtown Palo Alto, Redwood City Downtown, and Downtown San Mateo / Hayward Park are all useful because they answer different tenant decisions: premium downtown presence, mid-Peninsula civic/transit access, and practical professional/corridor office options.

South San Francisco should be explicitly biotech/flex/lab oriented. If the representative buildings do not support that identity, the page should wait until the building set is reviewed.
