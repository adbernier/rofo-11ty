## District Reviews

### Downtown Oakland

Raw corpus support:

- 804 assigned raw buildings.
- 1,725 historical listing rows.
- 702 high-confidence building assignments.
- Top decoded space type is office, followed by smaller retail context.
- Public-safe pattern: office-oriented district.

Assignment and drift review:

- Assignment quality is the strongest in this tranche.
- High-confidence assignments materially exceed medium-confidence assignments.
- The main drift issue is `2201 Broadway`, which raw extraction inherited as "Downtown Oakland" but current page data treats as Uptown Oakland. Keep it out of the Downtown Oakland representative set until boundary rules are cleaned.

Diversity/provenance review:

- Contact breadth is meaningful, but original company/provenance coverage is thin.
- LMS dominates listing rows. Treat LMS as Rofo's historical import/publishing layer, but require editorial review before promoting claims.

Recommended positioning:

Downtown Oakland is the East Bay's institutional business core: Broadway office buildings, City Center, civic institutions, BART access, and professional service demand in a formal downtown grid.

Editorial interpretation input:

> Downtown Oakland is the East Bay's formal business core: civic institutions, BART access, professional office buildings, and older downtown blocks clustered around Broadway and City Center.

It should fit legal, nonprofit, civic-adjacent, and professional service teams; organizations that need regional transit access; and companies comparing East Bay value with San Francisco CBD presence.

Nearby comparisons:

- Uptown Oakland: more mixed-use and smaller-company oriented, with stronger Uptown arts and retail context.
- Jack London Square: more waterfront and warehouse-adjacent, with service-commercial and adaptive texture.
- Old Oakland: smaller-scale historic blocks just west of the formal downtown core.
- San Francisco Financial District: more traditional regional CBD setting across the bay.

Representative examples:

- `1333 Broadway`
- `505 14th St`
- `300 Frank H Ogawa Plz`
- `1440 Broadway`

Flagged examples:

- `2201 Broadway`: assignment conflict between raw extraction and current public page data.

Map/image brief:

- Map should focus on Broadway, City Center, civic core, BART, Uptown, Jack London Square, Old Oakland, and Lake Merritt.
- Image should show Broadway, City Center, or civic-core office streetscape.

### Uptown Oakland

Raw corpus support:

- 1,093 assigned raw buildings.
- 1,252 historical listing rows.
- 461 high-confidence and 632 medium-confidence building assignments.
- Top decoded space type is office, with some retail and industrial context.
- Public-safe pattern: office-oriented, with mixed-use context handled editorially rather than as a raw claim.

Assignment and drift review:

- Medium-confidence assignments exceed high-confidence assignments, so boundary review is required.
- Some Lake Merritt and downtown-adjacent buildings may drift into Uptown because the district sits close to Downtown Oakland.
- Current representative examples are concentrated around Broadway, Franklin, and Webster.

Diversity/provenance review:

- Contact breadth is meaningful, but original company/provenance coverage is thin.
- LMS dominates listing rows. Keep this caveat internal.

Recommended positioning:

Uptown Oakland is the mixed-use office counterpart to Downtown Oakland: Broadway, Franklin, Webster, Lake Merritt, arts venues, food, apartments, and BART-accessible commercial blocks.

Editorial interpretation input:

> Uptown Oakland is the more mixed-use counterpart to Downtown Oakland, with office buildings, arts venues, food, apartments, BART access, and Lake Merritt-adjacent commercial blocks sharing a tighter urban setting.

It should fit smaller and mid-sized office users, creative/professional teams, nonprofits, and companies that want East Bay access with more street-level context than a formal CBD.

Nearby comparisons:

- Downtown Oakland: more formal, civic, and traditional office-core oriented.
- Jack London Square: more waterfront and service-commercial.
- Temescal: more neighborhood retail and small-business oriented.
- Berkeley: more university-adjacent and North East Bay oriented.

Representative examples:

- `1 Kaiser Plz`
- `2101 Webster St`
- `1970 Broadway`
- `415 20th St`

Flagged examples:

- `2201 Broadway`: good Uptown page example, but raw district naming conflict needs cleanup.
- `Lake Merritt Plaza`: strong raw candidate but no matching public building page found in current building output.

Map/image brief:

- Map should distinguish Uptown from Downtown and include Lake Merritt, Broadway, 19th St BART, Downtown Oakland, and Temescal.
- Image should show Broadway/Uptown mixed-use commercial blocks, not only Lake Merritt scenery.

### Downtown Palo Alto

Raw corpus support:

- 135 assigned raw buildings.
- 373 historical listing rows.
- 125 high-confidence assignments.
- Top decoded space type is office, with retail support.
- Public-safe pattern: office-oriented compact downtown.

Assignment and drift review:

- Assignment quality is compact and mostly high-confidence.
- Town & Country Village is useful context but sits at the district edge and should not anchor the page.
- California Avenue and Stanford Research Park should remain comparison/context areas, not part of the Downtown Palo Alto page.

Diversity/provenance review:

- Contact breadth is useful, but original company/provenance coverage is absent in this extract.
- LMS dominates listing rows. Keep source caveats internal.

Recommended positioning:

Downtown Palo Alto is a premium walkable Peninsula downtown for startup, venture-adjacent, professional service, and client-facing office users, grounded in University Avenue, Hamilton/Lytton blocks, retail, restaurants, and Caltrain access.

Editorial interpretation input:

> Downtown Palo Alto is a compact Peninsula business district where low-rise office buildings, restaurants, retail, Caltrain access, and startup/professional-service demand sit close together.

It should avoid Silicon Valley hype language. The emphasis should be walkability, client-facing presence, smaller professional office fabric, and comparison with R&D/campus alternatives.

Nearby comparisons:

- Mountain View / Castro-Whisman: more R&D and startup-corridor oriented.
- Redwood City Downtown: more civic and mid-Peninsula downtown oriented.
- Menlo Park: nearby affluent Peninsula alternative with smaller downtown and business-park context.
- California Avenue: more local and secondary commercial corridor.

Representative examples:

- `530 Lytton Ave`
- `228 Hamilton Ave`
- `200-228 Hamilton Ave`
- `400 Hamilton Ave`

Flagged examples:

- Town & Country Village: strong raw listing count, but edge-of-downtown and medium-confidence assignment.
- California Avenue buildings: useful nearby comparison context, not Downtown Palo Alto examples.

Map/image brief:

- Map should focus on University Avenue, Hamilton Avenue, Lytton Avenue, Caltrain, Menlo Park, Mountain View, Redwood City, and California Avenue.
- Image should show University/Hamilton/Lytton low-rise office and retail streetscape.

### South San Francisco Biotech Corridor

Raw corpus support:

- 1,298 assigned raw buildings.
- 936 historical listing rows.
- 923 high-confidence assignments.
- Top decoded space types are office and industrial, with a high public-safe industrial/flex pattern.
- This is the strongest differentiated non-office story in the tranche.

Assignment and drift review:

- Strong assignment support around Oyster Point, Gateway, Spruce, Airport Blvd, and related corridor addresses.
- Some medium-confidence corridor drift should be reviewed before launch.
- This page should not become a generic South San Francisco page.

Diversity/provenance review:

- Raw mix supports office plus industrial/flex context.
- Original provenance coverage is thin and LMS dominates listing rows.
- Public copy should describe durable geography, not availability or lab supply.

Recommended positioning:

South San Francisco Biotech Corridor should read as ecosystem geography: life science, lab-adjacent office, flex, industrial support, Oyster Point, Gateway, Lindenville, 101, and SFO access.

Editorial interpretation input:

> The South San Francisco Biotech Corridor is not a generic office market; it is a lab, R&D, flex, and industrial-support geography shaped by Oyster Point, Gateway, Lindenville, 101 access, and proximity to the airport.

It should fit life science, R&D, flex, and technical office users comparing Mission Bay with North Peninsula lab/flex access.

Nearby comparisons:

- Mission Bay: more urban and San Francisco-oriented life science setting.
- Redwood City Downtown: more downtown and professional-office oriented.
- Brisbane: more industrial and logistics-adjacent.
- Downtown Palo Alto: more walkable and client-facing, with less lab/flex orientation.

Representative examples:

- `90 S Spruce Ave`
- `11 Airport Blvd`
- `100 Produce Ave`

Flagged examples:

- `200 Linden Ave`: likely downtown retail context, weak for biotech corridor.
- `2228 Westborough Blvd`: likely too far from the Oyster Point/Gateway/Lindenville frame.
- Current public examples do not strongly express lab/life-science identity. Add or verify an Oyster Point/Gateway/lab/flex building before launch.

Map/image brief:

- Map should show Oyster Point, Gateway, Lindenville, 101, SFO, Mission Bay, Brisbane, and Redwood City.
- Image should show life science/flex campus or 101/Oyster Point/Gateway commercial context.

### Jack London Square

Raw corpus support:

- 404 assigned raw buildings.
- 589 historical listing rows.
- 276 high-confidence assignments.
- Top decoded space type is office, with retail and industrial support.
- Raw mix supports office plus waterfront/service-commercial texture.

Assignment and drift review:

- Strong candidates include Franklin, 3rd Street, Washington, and waterfront-edge addresses.
- Medium-confidence Linden/Embarcadero West examples may drift west and should be reviewed.
- Published representative depth is currently thin.

Diversity/provenance review:

- Contact breadth exists, but original provenance coverage is thin.
- LMS dominates listing rows. Keep this internal.

Recommended positioning:

Jack London Square is Oakland's waterfront commercial alternative: office and retail near the estuary, warehouse-adjacent and service-commercial blocks, ferry/rail/port access, and a less formal physical setting than Downtown or Uptown.

Editorial interpretation input:

> Jack London Square is Oakland's waterfront commercial district, where office and retail sit near warehouse-adjacent blocks, service-commercial streets, ferry access, rail lines, and the port edge.

It should not read as a generic downtown office district. The industrial/service context is part of the identity.

Nearby comparisons:

- Downtown Oakland: more formal, civic, and BART-oriented.
- Old Oakland: more historic downtown-adjacent.
- West Oakland: more industrial and logistics-oriented.
- Alameda: nearby waterfront, office, flex, and adaptive reuse alternative.

Representative examples:

- `66 Franklin St`
- `230 Madison St`
- `105 2nd St`

Flagged examples:

- Embarcadero West / Linden examples: medium-confidence; review before use.
- Generic downtown office examples: would make the district read like Downtown Oakland.

Map/image brief:

- Map should show the waterfront, Downtown Oakland, Old Oakland, West Oakland, Alameda, ferry, rail, I-880, and port edge.
- Image should show waterfront commercial, warehouse-adjacent, or Jack London office/retail context without becoming marina-only scenery.
