# Tier A Commercial Geography Expansion Plan

This planning pass extends the Atlanta editorial pilot into a curated Tier A rollout queue. It does not mass-generate pages. The recommendation is to expand by commercial district, not by raw neighborhood label, because many source labels are residential, overly broad, or building-complex-specific.

## Inventory audit summary

Current public neighborhood inventory is strongest in San Francisco, Oakland, Los Angeles, San Diego, Austin, Dallas, Miami, and Seattle. California, Texas, Florida, and Washington already have launched pages that can be refined toward the Atlanta reference model. Arizona, Oregon, and Nevada have meaningful historical city and building depth, but weaker public commercial-area structure.

Historical building depth is strongest in Sacramento, Phoenix, Austin, Dallas, Miami, Anaheim, Irvine, and several Bay Area cities. Candidate neighborhoods exist for many priority cities, but the best expansion candidates should be editorial composites where the commercial pattern is clearer than the raw source label.

## Data strength and diversity observations

The strongest near-term opportunities are not always the highest building-count cities. Sacramento and Phoenix have deep building inventory, but they need curated district segmentation. Irvine, Anaheim, Austin, Dallas, and Miami have both historical depth and clearer commercial district logic.

Several high-value editorial districts have weak or incomplete representative building inheritance. South Lake Union, Design District Miami, South Coast Metro, Newport Center, Emeryville, and Downtown Bellevue should be reviewed manually before launch so the visible examples support the commercial interpretation.

The Bay Area should be treated as the first curated expansion after Atlanta because it has strong Rofo relevance and unusually good comparison logic: Peninsula downtowns, East Bay transit cores, Oakland waterfront/adaptive districts, South San Francisco life science, and San Francisco central districts all reinforce one another.

Raw candidate labels should not be used directly for many markets. Phoenix, Scottsdale, Hayward, Marin, Portland, and Las Vegas include labels that are too residential, too broad, or too building-specific. These markets need editorial district naming and boundary review before public page generation.

## Suggested next implementation sequence

1. Create a curated Tier A district manifest for 20 to 30 districts, starting with Bay Area and Orange County.
2. For each district, define display name, city, state, commercial identity, nearby alternatives, representative building candidates, and optional image target path.
3. Upgrade existing public pages in Oakland, San Francisco, Austin, Dallas, Miami, and Seattle before adding many new pages.
4. Add the first new curated page batch: Downtown Palo Alto, Mountain View / Castro-Whisman, South San Francisco Biotech Corridor, Irvine Business Complex, Irvine Spectrum, Old Town Scottsdale, Phoenix Sky Harbor, Downtown Bellevue, Fort Lauderdale CBD, and Portland Pearl District.
5. Review representative building cards manually for each page before public rollout.
6. Add optional neighborhood imagery only where a specific image reinforces the district's commercial character.

## Not recommended for immediate rollout

- Generic residential neighborhood labels without commercial identity.
- Candidate pages that only represent a single building or business park name unless the district is widely recognized.
- Broad city quadrants such as "North Portland" or "South Fort Lauderdale" without a sharper commercial frame.
- Districts with no meaningful nearby alternatives.
- Pages where representative buildings all show the same asset type unless the commercial identity is intentionally narrow.
